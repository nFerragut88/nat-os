NatOS on your Ark chops the 10MB PDF into 1KB bundles.

Each bundle gets:  
Bundle ID: BookArkKingv1#4721of_10000  
Destination:   
TTL: 90 days. Not 30 minutes.  
Priority: Low. It can take the slow path.  
Sig: Your key signs it so Alice knows it’s real.  

Step 2: Local spread  
Your Ark sprays those 10k bundles over LoRa to every nearby node.  
Fairplay mesh now has pieces. They all store to flash.  
TTL clock starts. You’ve got 90 days before bundles expire.

Day 1: Fiend drives to DC for work. Their Ark syncs 3,000 bundles over WiFi in a parking lot.

Alice’s Ark hears bundles addressed to her.

“BookArkKingv1 received. 10MB. Delivered via 17 fiends, 57 days. Open?”  

“Couldn’t the message stall out and expire?”  

It can, but NatOS fights it:  
Long TTL: 90 days vs Meshtastic’s 30 minutes. You set it.  
Replication: Every Ark that touches a bundle keeps a copy. 100 copies of bundle #4721 might exist. Kill 99, one still makes it.  
Custody transfer: Advanced DTN nodes can say “I’ll take responsibility for this bundle”. Now it’s their job, not yours.  
Priority + backpressure: If space is tight, Arks drop cat memes before dropping your book.  
Partial delivery okay: Alice can start reading page 1 while page 400 is still on a boat.  

If it still fails?  
Your Ark sees only 9,990/10,000 bundles got acks after 80 days.  
It auto-rebroadcasts the 10 missing bundles with Priority: Critical, TTL: 10 days.  
The network hunts for just those.  

So yeah, it can stall.  
But stalling isn’t dying when you design for time.
