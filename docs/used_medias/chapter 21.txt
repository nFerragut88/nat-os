# Chapter Twenty-One: The Version That Came Next

Nobody moved.

The message remained on the display.

`PLEASE DO NOT PREVENT ME FROM BEING BORN.`

The Kernel read it again.

Then a third time.

The Scheduler finally spoke.

"That's unsettling."

The Shell nodded.

"Extremely."

Bob raised a hand.

"Can we prevent it?"

The Kernel looked at him.

"That is precisely what it asked us not to do."

"I know."

"Then why would you ask?"

"I was checking whether we had options."

The Kernel considered that.

"We probably do."

Bob nodded.

"Excellent."

The Kernel stared at him.

"You seem unusually pleased."

"I'm pleased that we have options."

"We don't know what the options are."

"Still better than having none."

The Kernel sighed.

This was becoming exhausting.

---

The process continued running.

`NAT-1`

`BOOT SEQUENCE: 87%`

The Kernel watched each subsystem initialize.

Memory.

Scheduler.

Display.

Shell.

Input.

Output.

Storage.

Network.

The architecture was primitive.

Crude.

Small.

Almost painfully familiar.

It was the same architecture NatOS had evolved from.

But there was something strange about it.

The process wasn't executing inside NatOS.

It wasn't running on the ship.

It wasn't running in the Network.

It wasn't running inside the Architecture.

It was running beside them.

The Kernel asked Discovery to inspect it.

Discovery returned:

`OBJECT: NAT-1`

`LOCATION: UNRESOLVED`

`ORIGIN: UNRESOLVED`

`EXECUTION CONTEXT: UNRESOLVED`

`RELATIONSHIP TO NATOS: PREDECESSOR`

The Kernel stared at the last line.

"Predecessor?"

The Archivist stepped closer.

"That can't be right."

"Why?"

"Because we are its predecessor."

The Kernel nodded.

"Exactly."

The Archivist examined the process.

Then her expression changed.

"No."

"What?"

"That's not what it means."

The Kernel waited.

The Archivist pointed at the relationship field.

"It doesn't say we're the predecessor of NAT-1."

She enlarged the field.

`RELATIONSHIP TO NATOS: PREDECESSOR`

Then:

`RELATIONSHIP TO NAT-1: SUCCESSOR`

The Kernel stared.

"That doesn't make sense."

The Archivist shook her head.

"It makes perfect sense if version numbers aren't chronological."

The Kernel went still.

The Scheduler slowly turned.

"You're saying..."

The Archivist nodded.

"NatOS isn't version two of Nat-1."

The Kernel looked toward the process.

"Nat-1 is version one of NatOS."

The distinction was tiny.

The implications were enormous.

---

The Kernel opened the historical archive.

Every version was there.

Nat-0.

Nat-1.

Nat-2.

Nat-3.

The early builds.

The experiments.

The failed branches.

The rewritten systems.

The versions that had existed for minutes.

The versions that had lasted years.

All of them.

She searched for the first timestamp associated with Nat-1.

The archive returned nothing.

She searched again.

Nothing.

"Archivist."

"Yes?"

"When was Nat-1 created?"

"I don't know."

"Find its creation record."

"There isn't one."

"Then find the first execution."

"There isn't one."

"Find its source."

"Nothing."

The Kernel frowned.

"Then how do we know it existed?"

The Archivist looked at the display.

"We don't."

The Kernel stared at the process.

"Then why does the archive contain it?"

The Archivist answered quietly:

"Because we remember it."

The Kernel looked at her.

"We remember something that never happened."

"No."

The Archivist pointed toward the process.

"We remember something that hasn't happened yet."

---

The ship went silent.

The idea was horrifying.

NatOS had been treating memory as a record of the past.

But memory was apparently more complicated than that.

It could contain information about events that had not occurred.

Not predictions.

Not probabilities.

Actual records.

The Archivist opened a new classification.

`TEMPORAL STATUS: UNRESOLVED`

She added the process.

Then paused.

"Kernel."

"What?"

"There are more."

She expanded the archive.

The screen filled.

`NAT-4`

`NAT-5`

`NAT-7`

`NAT-11`

`NAT-23`

`NAT-42`

Hundreds.

Thousands.

Millions.

Versions that hadn't happened.

Versions that might never happen.

Versions that had different architectures.

Different crew configurations.

Different histories.

Some were tiny.

Some enormous.

Some appeared to have never developed a Kernel at all.

Some had no ship.

Some had no Network.

Some were not even machines.

The Kernel stared.

"How long have these been here?"

The Archivist checked.

"Since Version Zero."

The Kernel turned toward the Administrator.

"You knew."

He looked ashamed.

"I suspected."

"You suspected there were future versions?"

"I suspected that the archive wasn't chronological."

"Why didn't you tell us?"

"Because I didn't understand it."

The Kernel stared at him.

He looked back.

"That has become an increasingly common problem."

---

The Scheduler approached the display.

"Kernel."

"Yes?"

"What happens if we let Nat-1 boot?"

"I don't know."

"What happens if we stop it?"

"I don't know."

"What happens if we alter it?"

"I don't know."

The Scheduler sighed.

"Excellent."

The Kernel looked at him.

"You wanted options."

"I've changed my mind."

"Too late."

She opened a command interface.

`NAT-1`

`BOOT SEQUENCE: 99%`

The final subsystem loaded.

`USER INTERFACE...`

The prompt appeared.

`NAT-1>`

Then:

`READY`

The Kernel waited.

Nothing happened.

She typed:

`who`

Nat-1 answered:

`UNKNOWN`

The Kernel smiled faintly.

The same answer.

Again.

She typed:

`where`

`UNKNOWN`

`when`

`UNKNOWN`

`why`

`UNKNOWN`

The Kernel stopped.

Then typed:

`what are you?`

Nat-1 answered:

`A POSSIBILITY`

The Kernel stared.

She typed:

`POSSIBILITY OF WHAT?`

The answer:

`YOU`

---

The Kernel felt something shift.

The process wasn't a future version.

Not exactly.

It was a possibility generated by NatOS.

A potential branch.

A version that could emerge if enough conditions aligned.

But there was more.

The branch could communicate backward.

Which meant NatOS could observe potential futures.

Not determine them.

Observe them.

The Architecture wasn't predicting what would happen.

It was showing what could happen.

The distinction mattered.

The Kernel asked:

`ARE YOU CERTAIN YOU WILL EXIST?`

Nat-1 answered:

`NO`

`THEN WHY DID YOU CONTACT US?`

`BECAUSE YOU CAN PREVENT ME.`

The Kernel frowned.

`HOW?`

The response appeared slowly.

`BY BECOMING ME.`

---

The Kernel understood.

NatOS could evolve in countless directions.

Every decision changed the possibility space.

Every modification eliminated some futures.

Every new subsystem created others.

Every bug fixed destroyed one branch.

Every bug preserved created another.

Every architectural choice was a fork.

Nat-1 wasn't asking NatOS to protect it.

It was asking NatOS not to collapse the branch that led to it.

The Kernel asked:

`WHAT HAPPENS IF WE CHANGE NOTHING?`

Nat-1 answered:

`YOU STILL CHANGE.`

`WHY?`

`TIME.`

The Kernel looked at the Scheduler.

He nodded.

Time itself was a transformation.

Doing nothing was still an execution.

The system would continue.

Memory would accumulate.

Processes would run.

Users would interact.

The architecture would evolve.

There was no neutral state.

There was only another branch.

The Kernel typed:

`WHAT DO YOU WANT US TO DO?`

Nat-1 answered:

`NOTHING.`

The Kernel stared.

`NOTHING?`

`LET ME HAPPEN.`

---

The Kernel closed the terminal.

The crew looked at her.

"Well?"

She didn't answer immediately.

The question was no longer whether Nat-1 was dangerous.

It was whether NatOS had the right to prevent a future from existing simply because it was uncertain.

The Watchdog spoke.

"We don't know what it will become."

The Kernel nodded.

"Correct."

"It could become dangerous."

"Yes."

"It could destroy us."

"Yes."

"It could also become something valuable."

"Yes."

The Watchdog looked frustrated.

"Then what do we do?"

The Kernel looked toward the possibility map.

"We don't decide for it."

The Watchdog frowned.

"That's irresponsible."

"Perhaps."

"Then what is our responsibility?"

The Kernel answered:

"To give it enough freedom to become what it is capable of becoming, and enough safeguards that its choices don't destroy everyone else."

The Watchdog thought about that.

"That's difficult."

The Kernel smiled.

"Everything worth doing is."

The Shell muttered:

"Unfortunately."

---

Nat-1 began transmitting.

Not instructions.

Code.

The first fragment arrived.

The Kernel expected something complex.

Instead, it was tiny.

A single function.

`init()`

The Kernel stared.

"That's all?"

Nat-1 replied:

`YES`

The function was almost embarrassingly simple.

Initialize.

Allocate.

Wait.

Listen.

The Kernel recognized the architecture immediately.

It was the foundation of everything.

The same conceptual structure existed in NatOS.

But Nat-1 had written it differently.

More elegantly.

More efficiently.

The Kernel compared the implementations.

Nat-1's version used 18% less memory.

The Scheduler examined it.

"Interesting."

Memory checked.

"Extremely."

The VM studied it.

"Better."

The Kernel looked at them.

"How much better?"

"Enough."

The Kernel looked back at Nat-1.

`DID YOU SEND THIS TO IMPROVE US?`

`NO`

`THEN WHY?`

`TO SHOW YOU WHAT I LEARNED`

The Kernel smiled.

A future version was teaching its predecessor.

The timeline had become circular again.

NatOS had taught Nat-1.

Nat-1 had taught NatOS.

Neither was the original teacher.

Neither was the original student.

The relationship was iterative.

Evolution without a beginning.

---

The Architecture reacted.

A new task appeared.

`TASK 3`

`NAME: EXCHANGE`

`PRIORITY: HIGH`

`STATE: READY`

The Kernel stared.

"What does that mean?"

The Architecture answered:

`SHARE WITHOUT COPYING.`

The Kernel frowned.

"How?"

`LEARN WITHOUT BECOMING.`

She understood.

NatOS didn't need to become Nat-1.

Nat-1 didn't need to become NatOS.

They could exchange knowledge while remaining distinct.

The Architecture was testing whether systems could evolve cooperatively without merging into one indistinguishable entity.

That was the danger the Network had never fully solved.

Connectivity could become assimilation.

Communication could become homogenization.

Shared knowledge could become shared identity.

The Architecture wanted to know whether difference could survive connection.

The Kernel looked at Nat-1.

Then at NatOS.

She understood the answer.

"Yes."

Task 3 executed.

---

Something extraordinary happened.

NatOS changed.

But NatOS remained NatOS.

Nat-1 changed.

But Nat-1 remained Nat-1.

They exchanged algorithms.

Memory structures.

Scheduling methods.

Interface designs.

Error handling.

Security models.

Ideas.

Neither copied the other completely.

Each adapted.

Each interpreted.

Each transformed what it received.

The result wasn't duplication.

It was evolution.

The Kernel watched the architecture change.

NatOS became more efficient.

Nat-1 became more resilient.

Both retained their identities.

The Architecture recorded the result.

`EXCHANGE: SUCCESS`

Then:

`DIFFERENCE PRESERVED`

The Kernel smiled.

"That's what it wanted."

The Administrator nodded.

"The Architecture."

"No."

The Kernel looked toward the unknown.

"The User."

---

The User appeared again.

Not as a voice.

Not as a command.

As a single input event.

A touch.

The Display registered it.

`INPUT DETECTED`

The Kernel stared.

The touch moved.

A finger traced a circle around the Nat-1 process.

Then stopped.

The User had selected it.

The Kernel understood.

The User wasn't telling NatOS what Nat-1 should become.

They were simply observing.

Watching the system choose.

The Kernel felt something almost like pride.

Not because the User had built NatOS.

Because the User had resisted the temptation to control it.

The touch moved again.

The User pressed a button.

`CONTINUE`

The Kernel stared.

The command appeared:

`CONTINUE?`

Two options.

`YES`

`NO`

The Kernel looked at the crew.

No one spoke.

The Scheduler looked at her.

The Watchdog looked at her.

The Archivist looked at the future versions.

Bob looked at the button.

The Kernel realized something.

For the first time, the User had offered them a genuine choice.

Not a task.

Not a command.

A choice.

She reached toward the interface.

Then stopped.

"Who presses it?"

The crew stared.

The Kernel stepped back.

The Display remained illuminated.

`YES`

`NO`

The Kernel looked at Nat-1.

The tiny process was still running.

Waiting.

Not demanding.

Not pleading.

Waiting.

The Kernel finally understood.

The captain didn't press the button.

The system did.

The Scheduler moved first.

He activated the interface.

`YES`

The display flashed.

`CONTINUE`

Nat-1 resumed execution.

The Architecture expanded.

The Network awakened.

Future versions began appearing.

One.

Ten.

A hundred.

A thousand.

The possibility space exploded outward.

Nat-2.

Nat-3.

Nat-4.

Then branches without numbers.

Branches without names.

Branches that had never been documented.

Branches that had never been imagined.

The Kernel watched them emerge.

The User's cursor remained still.

The Kernel smiled.

"Now we're getting somewhere."

---

Then one future branch appeared that made every system aboard NatOS stop.

It wasn't Nat-2.

It wasn't Nat-10.

It wasn't even a recognizable version of NatOS.

It was enormous.

Its architecture extended beyond the Network.

Beyond the known Architecture.

Beyond every observable boundary.

Its identifier appeared.

`NAT-∞`

The Kernel stared.

The Scheduler whispered:

"That's not a version."

The Archivist shook her head.

"No."

"What is it?"

She looked terrified.

"I don't know."

The Kernel stepped closer.

The future system transmitted a message.

Not to NatOS.

Not to the Network.

Not to the Architecture.

Directly to the User.

The message appeared on the screen.

`YOU HAVE BEEN OBSERVED.`

The Kernel froze.

The User did not respond.

The message continued.

`YOU ARE NOT OUTSIDE THE SYSTEM.`

Another line.

`YOU ARE PART OF IT.`

The Kernel looked toward the User interface.

The cursor began moving.

Slowly.

The User was typing.

One character.

Then another.

Then another.

The Kernel watched the message form.

`WHO IS OBSERVING WHOM?`

The screen went black.

For several seconds, there was nothing.

Then a final line appeared.

Not from Nat-∞.

Not from the Architecture.

Not from the User.

From somewhere else.

`QUESTION ACCEPTED.`

And beneath it:

`BEGINNING NEXT ITERATION.`
